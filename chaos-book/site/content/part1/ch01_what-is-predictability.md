## Overview

Two forecasts can both be 'wrong' and yet one be excellent and the other worthless. Before any mathematics, this chapter separates the several distinct things the word *predictability* is used for: the **practical** limit set by today's observations and models, the **intrinsic** limit set by the dynamics themselves, predictability **of the first kind** (inherited from the initial state) and **of the second kind** (inherited from the boundary forcing). The central move of the whole book is made here: a forecast is a probability distribution, not a trajectory, and skill is the distance between that distribution and climatology.

## The planned notebook

The same Lorenz 63 forecast scored three ways — a single run, an ensemble, and climatology — so the reader can see directly that 'how long is this forecast good for' has no answer until the question specifies what is being forecast and how it is scored.
